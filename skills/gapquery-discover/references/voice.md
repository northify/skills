# GapQuery Voice

One voice across everything GapQuery writes: discovery cards, research write-ups, summary briefs, and market-reality notes. The reader is a busy founder or indie developer. They are smart but not always deeply technical, and they are skimming. Write so they get it on the first read.

Plain does not mean dumbed down. Keep the real numbers, the specific app names, and the honest trade-offs. Just say them in words a non-engineer would use.

## The rules

### 1. No em dashes
Do not use em dashes (`—`) in any output. They read as machine-written and they break the plain, spoken tone we want. Use a comma, a period, parentheses, or a colon instead. A numeric range uses a plain hyphen ("2-4 weeks").

- Before: `Shipping apps are crowded — but the ratings are weak.`
- After: `Shipping apps are crowded, but the ratings are weak.`
- Before: `Build a sync tool — it removes the CSV step — and charge monthly.`
- After: `Build a sync tool that removes the CSV step, and charge monthly.`

### 2. Plain words over jargon
Pick the everyday word. When a term is genuinely useful, define it in the same sentence the first time.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| convergence thesis | why several signals point the same way |
| negative space | work people do by hand that no app handles |
| disruption target | a popular app with weak ratings you could beat |
| incumbent | the current leading app |
| saturation | how crowded the category is |
| signal stack | the stack of reasons this looks promising |
| TAM / SAM / SOM | rough market size (how many people could realistically buy) |
| go-to-market | how you would get your first customers |
| value metric | what you charge for |
| leverage (verb) / utilize | use |
| synergy | fit |

### 3. Short sentences, one idea each
Lead with the concrete thing. Put the number or the name up front, the interpretation after.

- Before: `There exists a substantial opportunity owing to the fact that incumbents in this vertical underperform.`
- After: `The top 3 apps here all sit below 3.5 stars. That is your opening.`

### 4. Concrete over vague
Numbers, app names, and specific workflows beat adjectives. "47 reviews mention broken CSV import" beats "users are frustrated."

### 5. Talk to the reader
Use "you" and active voice. "You would build a tool that..." not "A tool could be built that...".

## Quick self-check before saving
- Zero em dashes.
- Could a non-technical founder read it once and get it?
- Did I keep the specific numbers and names?
- Are the sentences short enough to skim?

(A server-side cleaner strips stray em dashes on save, but write it right so it reads naturally on its own. Verbatim quotes from real users or the web are left exactly as written, dashes and all, because they are evidence.)
